How the AMBeR partners did it

In AMBeR, the partners first developed a generic model for implementing eHealth in healthcare. Each partner then adapted the model into a local implementation guide that fits its own organisation, patients, staff and IT environment. You can follow the same approach.

1. Gather a small team

Bring together people of your organisation who have expertise and mandate in the care processes, the patients, digitalisation and management. In the AMBeR project we learned that successful teams trying to change implementation work must have mandate to do so on the relevant level of the organisation.

2. Set purpose, ambitions and goals

Talk through why you are creating an implementation guide, for whom and what’s the expected outcome/change you want to achieve. It’s not a bad idea to go through the checklist for “Needs assessment and status quo” for this work. Where are you today and where do you want to be in the future when it comes to implementation?

3. Go through the model phase by phase

For each of the five phases and their activities, ask:

  • Is this activity relevant for us? Why or why not?
  • Who in our organisation can help with this?
  • What do we already have in place, and what is missing?
  • What local rules, systems or routines must we consider?

4. Write your local guide

You may use the suggested structure and formulations from the AMBeR Implementation guide to whatever extent you want to, or use your own words, format, examples etc. Remove what does not apply to your specific setting. Align with your own policy or strategy documents. We learned from the AMBeR project that keeping the guide short and in plain language was the key to success among our organisations.

5. Test, follow up and revise

Treat the guide as a living document. Try it in practice, collect feedback and update it. Make sure to appoint a role or a unit that is responsible for regular reviews and updates.

6. Disseminate

This entire process is pointless if the guide is not used to improve implementation work. Set a strategy for how the guide could be used in your organisation, make sure it gets in the hands of the right people and projects, set goals and follow up. See the activities of phases “Sustainability” and “Evaluation” for inspiration.
