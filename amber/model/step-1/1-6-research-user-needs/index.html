The implementation model / Phase 1 / Activity 1.6

Activity 1.6

Research User Needs

Users can be patients, clinicians or support staff. Their experiences, needs and everyday reality must guide any solution. Early knowledge about how they act, what challenges they have and what motivates them gives direction and helps make solutions relevant, acceptable and possible in practice.

Without this knowledge, even a well-designed solution can miss real needs.

Checklist

  • Define the main user groups, for example patients, nurses, doctors and administrative staff.
  • Choose methods to learn about the needs and situation of each group.
  • Carry out interviews, observations or surveys, and write down the findings in a structured way.
  • Observe users in their real work environment when possible.
  • Look for repeated themes, problems, needs and workarounds.
  • Share and check the findings with the team and stakeholders.
  • Use the findings to describe the requirements and the problem.

Do you want to download the checklists and learn how to customise them for your own organisation? Go to Create your local guide.

Methods and tools

  • Interviews and focus groups: Talk to one person or a small group to learn about their experiences and needs.
  • Observation at the workplace (field study): Watch people work in their normal setting.
  • Surveys and questionnaires: Short questionnaires to many people.
  • Diary study: Users note their experiences over time.
  • Personas: Short descriptions of typical users, based on research.
  • Affinity diagram: Groups notes from interviews and observations into themes.
