Northviken Health and Care Centre is a fictional public integrated health facility with specialist outpatient and inpatient care, emergency care, rehabilitation, and home and digital care. It serves a mixed urban, peri-urban and rural population of about 55,000 people, within a larger regional health authority.

It is built to hold almost any kind of care: cancer care, maternity care, psychiatry, rehabilitation and more. It belongs to no country, so that people in every partner country can recognise it as their own.

The name may be adapted to each language. Swedish: Nordviken.

What Northviken is

  • Public, mid-sized and integrated: outpatient, inpatient, emergency, home and digital care
  • Part of a regional health authority with central IT and digital governance
  • Working under a national eHealth strategy it did not design
  • Serving people with widely varying digital skills, languages and physical abilities
  • At digital readiness level 2: some things are digital, the picture at the front line is unclear

What it is not

  • A large university hospital
  • Private or insurance-funded
  • Digitally advanced: DEAHL-Connect is its first integrated patient-facing platform
  • In crisis: things mostly work, but not as well as they could
  • Located in any real country, city or region

The story starts here

Eight weeks after DEAHL-Connect went live, the procurement is done, the training has happened and the figures look acceptable. But the conditions for confident, safe and equal use have not been built.

The story does not begin with a crisis. It begins with the quiet, cumulative cost of the gap, visible in what happens to particular people on particular days.

Ingrid, 64, gets an SMS asking her to fill in a questionnaire before her rheumatology follow-up. She gives up halfway. At the appointment, Dr. Amara has no time to do more than explain one scale. That evening, Karen adds two figures to the implementation report: questionnaire completion 68 per cent, clinician log-in 91 per cent.
